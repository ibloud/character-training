The research should also ask who benefits from changing that language and whose experience it leaves out.

Research questions

  • How do healthcare, palliative care, hospice and end-of-life decisions connect or differ in the selected jurisdiction? Who pays, who decides and who bears unpaid work?
  • What do estate planning, probate and administration cost, and which costs are public fees versus professional services? Separate them from medical treatment and funeral expenditure.
  • What are the relevant burial, cremation, funeral and memorial services, their pricing practices and differences across traditions?
  • What counts as bereavement support? Distinguish clinical services, charities, peer/community support, workplace policy and commercial products.
  • Beyond expenditure, what burdens may include time, fatigue, unpaid care, administration, lost earnings, disrupted relationships, cultural exclusion or loss of agency? Which are measured, reported or only hypothesized?
  • Could an original story or facilitated reading encounter help people ask questions? What study would test comprehension, comfort or agency without claiming treatment?

Evidence ledger and double-counting rules

For each numerical claim record source URL/document, publisher, publication date, period covered, geography, currency and price year, population, sector definition, metric, method, limitations and verification date. Distinguish industry revenue, provider profit, household expenditure, public spending, insurance payments, employment and unpaid care. Map payer-to-provider flows before adding totals: the same payment can appear in several datasets. Never sum overlapping sectors into a headline “death industry” market. Separate sector scale from a reachable buyer set and the project’s own negotiated revenue.

No sector revenue estimates, causal conclusions or project forecasts have been verified for this package. Choose jurisdiction and period before commissioning the analysis. Candidate source families: national statistics, public health/payer agencies, official fee schedules, consumer regulators, peer-reviewed research and clearly identified trade surveys. Trade evidence needs its sample and commercial interests recorded.

Original reading/reflection prompts

Choose one; participation can remain private. Use a fictional situation if preferred. 1. Who has authority to make a decision in this passage, and who carries its consequences? 2. Which costs does the narrator count? Which time, labor or relationship costs remain outside the account? 3. What changes when a decision is framed as care, inheritance, duty or a purchase? 4. What would PIXIE ask before offering help? What assumption would Death’s Daughter challenge? Reverse those roles and see whether the scene improves. 5. Where does the comparison to a real service fail? Name the missing evidence instead of diagnosing a person or institution.

Star Chamber influence — stewardship, not secrecy

Owner-supplied cultural reference: The Star Chamber, associated with Michael Douglas. Verify film credits and the exact thematic reference before a public critical essay. Record the influence as a question: who watches those who claim to protect us? Translate it into original gold/plum imagery, competing viewpoints and visible decision records, not lifted film dialogue, imagery or implied endorsement.

Do not invent an actual Star Chamber board. For any real review group, agree remit, members, consent, compensation, conflicts, decision criteria, confidentiality boundaries and a route for correction/appeal. Private personal evidence can remain confidential while the process and authority are understandable. A literary frame does not grant clinical, legal or commercial authority.
